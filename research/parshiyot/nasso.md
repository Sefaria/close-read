## 1. Corpus inventory

Nechama wrote **28 sheets on Parshat Nasso** between 1942 and 1971 — 30 years of returns.
Each sheet anchors on one or two passages within the parsha; she rarely repeats herself
verbatim, but she returns to the same questions across decades, sharpening or shifting
the angle.

The sheets cluster into **six themes**. Counts and the year-range tell you where her
attention sat:

| # | Theme | Verses | Sheets | Years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **גזל הגר** — Theft from the convert | Num 5:5–10 | 5 | 1944–1968 |
| 2 | **פרשת נזיר** — The Nazirite | Num 6:1–21 | 4 | 1942–1970 |
| 3 | **ברכת כהנים** — Priestly Blessing | Num 6:22–27 | 6 | 1943–1971 |
| 4 | **קרבן/קרבנות הנשיאים** — Princes' offerings | Num 7:1–89 | 7 | 1949–1969 |
| 5 | **מפקד הלויים** — Census of the Levites | Num 4 | 3 | 1945–1965 |
| 6 | **הפטרה — סיפור שמשון/מנוח** — Haftarah / Samson | Judges 13 | 2 | 1959–1963 |
| — | טומאת זב ומצורע (Tumah of zav/metzora) | Num 5:1–4 | 1 | 1952 (lone outlier) |

**Conspicuous absence:** there is no Sotah sheet. With 28 sheets across 30 years she never
once made the trial of the suspected adulteress the headline of a gilayon. (Worth noting
in the overview narration — it's a real datum about what she found teachable.)

## 2. Sample render — one sheet as a Close Read leaf

To validate that the workflow produces something faithful, here is **how the 1944 גזל הגר
sheet (id 161971) translates** into close-read steps. This is the raw structure; the JSON
draft will polish wording.

**Sheet:** `פרשת נשא תש"ד - גזל הגר` (1944). Anchor: Numbers 5:5–10.
She moves through **7 cruxes**. We'd render **3–4 of them** (the most pedagogically rich)
as a single leaf — not all 7. The rest live in the pack as "if-extended."

| Sheet section | Verse focus | Sources Nechama chose | Her question | → Close-read step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| א. דין קרן וחומש בשבועת שקר | Num 5:6–7 | Rambam, Hilkhot Gezeila ve-Aveida 7:1, 7:8 | Find the basis in our verses for the law of Hil. 7:8 (only `mode' me'atzmo` pays the fifth) | narration → commentary (Rambam) → narration → question |
| ב. תנאי ותוצאה | Num 5:6 | (none — pure verse) | Where does the *condition* end and the *result* begin in this verse? Justify your answer. | narration → question (highlight whole verse) |
| ג. הוראת הסמיכות "חטאות האדם" | Num 5:6 + Ex 28:38 + Lev 5:22 | parallel verses | Two construct-state readings: "*sins of* humans" vs "*sins against* humans." Which fits the verse? | narration → 2 commentary cards (parallel verses) → question |
| ד. "למעול מעל בה'" | Num 5:6 | Sifra (R. Akiva) | (Sifra: false oath in a private deposit) What is R. Akiva's idea, and what difficulty in the verse does it resolve? | narration → commentary (Sifra/R. Akiva) → question |
| ה. וידוי דברים נלמד מפרשתנו | Num 5:7 | Rambam, Hil. Teshuvah 1:1; Lev 5:5 | Why is the mitzvah of verbal confession derived from *here* and not from Lev 5:5 which already said `vehitvada`? | narration → commentary (Rambam) → narration (Lev 5:5) → question |
| ו. שאלות ודיוקים ברש"י | Num 5:7–10 | Rashi on 5:7, 5:8(×4), 5:10(×2); Ibn Ezra on 5:8 | A cluster of Rashi-precision questions, including the famous "is there any Israelite who has no kinsman?" | narration → 3–4 commentary cards (Rashi sequence + IE) → question |
| ז. ישראל המחזיק בנכסי הגר | Num 5:8 | Rashi on 5:8; Rambam Hil. Zechiyah 1:6 | Why doesn't the finder keep the heirless convert's property — why must it go to the priest? | narration → commentary (Rambam) → question |

**Picking the leaf:** for the 1944 leaf I'd render **א + ד + ו** — they're the famous moves
and they cohere: principal-vs-fifth law, the false oath as trespass against God, and the
Rashi sequence ending in "and the convert has no kin." A user gets a full Nechama in
about 12 cards.

## 3. Proposed structure for `data/nasso.json`

A two-level branching garden whose **shape grows from her oeuvre**: themes (her clusters)
at Level 0, individual gilyonot (her angles across years) at Level 1.

### 3.1 The frame

The overview names the corpus honestly: 28 sheets, six themes, three decades, the absence
of Sotah. The reader gets the lay of the garden before walking into it.

### 3.2 Level 0 — six themes (or four)

**Recommended:** ship with **four themes** initially (Gezel, Nazir, Birkat, Nesi'im — the
canonical clusters everyone associates with Nasso), and **add Levites and Haftarah-Samson
as a second pass** once we've proven the workflow. Reason: Levites (3 sheets) and
Haftarah (2) yield smaller branches with thinner content; the four canonical themes have
21 of the 28 sheets and the strongest interpretive material.

If you want all six at launch, that's a 2x3 grid at Level 0 (the engine already supports it).

### 3.3 Level 1 — selected gilyonot per theme

Not every sheet becomes a leaf. The selection criterion: spread across decades (so the
reader feels her return to the question over time), at least one sheet with multi-crux
density, and pedagogical contrast between the chosen sheets.

**Recommended selection (12 leaves):**

| Theme | Year picks | Why this trio |
|---|---|---|
| Gezel HaGer | **1944**, **1953**, **1968** | 1944 = the foundational long-form treatment (7 cruxes, classic Rambam-Rashi-Sifra setup) · 1953 = pivot to *love of the convert* as the moral center (אהבת הגר) · 1968 = the late return, asking new questions (the מלגו/מלבר debate on the fifth) |
| Nazir | **1942**, **1946**, **1970** | 1942 = early canonical · 1946 = mid-century, where she frames the sinner/saint problem · 1970 = the late essay form (with English) — a glimpse of how she taught the question in late life |
| Birkat Kohanim | **1951**, **1955**, **1962** | 1951 = the "what can humans bless" frame · 1955 = her densest exposition (5 cruxes, one per verse word) · 1962 = the structure-of-the-parsha sheet — where she places Birkat Kohanim in the architecture of Nasso |
| Nesi'im | **1957**, **1960**, **1966**, **1969** | 1957 = the founding "why repeat 12x?" question · 1960 = her densest sheet on this (11 cruxes) · 1966 = Ibn Kaspi as the centerpiece · 1969 = the late return, hooking back into 1949's "kalot Moshe" reading |

Total: **3 × 3 + 1 × 4 = 13 leaves.** (Nesi'im is her heaviest topic; it earns four.)

### 3.4 Within each leaf — render her actual sheet

Each leaf is a faithful rendering of *that gilayon's* internal structure:

1. **Sheet-opening narration card** — names the year, the sheet number, the verse she's
   working on. Example: "Gilayon תשי"ג / 1953. Numbers 5:5–10. Nechama returns to *gezel
   hager* and this time the headline is *the love of the convert.*"
2. **3–5 of her cruxes** rendered as: narration → her chosen commentary card(s) → her question
   (as a `question` step with `questionLabel: "Nechama asks"`). Pick the most pedagogically
   rich cruxes; skip the technical-grammar ones unless they're the point.
3. **Her closing question** — almost every sheet ends with a "leave the reader sitting
   with something" question. That becomes the leaf's terminal `question` card.
4. **(Optional carry-forward card)** — a modern voice that takes up her question (Rav Kook,
   Rav Hirsch, a Hasidic source) if and only if the corpus supports it. Many of her
   sheets don't carry forward; resist the urge to invent one.

### 3.5 Narrator voice — narrower

The narrator names her, names the year of the sheet, names which commentator is up next,
and gets out of the way. **No more narrator-as-pseudo-commentator.** All interpretive
moves come from the sources Nechama herself chose; the narrator's job is to make her
editorial choices visible.

## 4. What this changes in the close-read-sheet skill

The Pirkei Avot / Yitro / Shemini sheets use a different arc (Mode 1 or Mode 2). Nasso
and its successors use a new mode:

**Mode 4 — Nechama parsha** (proposed addition to [pedagogical-arcs.md](../../.claude/skills/close-read-sheet/references/pedagogical-arcs.md)):

- Inventory her sheets for the parsha (via Sefaria user 54380 sheets API).
- Build a per-parsha research pack (this file).
- Level 0 fork = her themes. Level 1 fork = her individual gilyonot, dated.
- Each leaf renders one gilayon's actual structure.
- Add `Nechama Leibowitz` as a recognized `source` token (one CSS line in `close-read.css`).

This is the only deliverable beyond the JSON itself: a small skill update so this becomes
a one-skill-invocation per parsha next time.

## 5. Open choice points

1. **Four themes or six at launch?** Recommended four (Gezel, Nazir, Birkat, Nesi'im).
   The other two can come in v2 without touching the rest.
2. **3 leaves per theme or 4?** Recommended 3 for Gezel/Nazir/Birkat; 4 for Nesi'im (her
   heaviest topic). Total 13. Could trim to 12 by dropping one Nesi'im leaf.
3. **English handling.** Some of her sheets have English (1970 Nazir specifically);
   most don't. For the close-read, Hebrew sources stay bilingual via Sefaria's existing
   translations of the *commentators she cites* (Rashi etc. have English on Sefaria);
   her own framing/questions get translated by us, narrator-style, English-only.
4. **Hebrew of her own voice.** Her gilayon questions are in Hebrew. The questions are
   often quite long and discursive. Recommendation: render in *English-only* narration
   cards, attributed ("Nechama asks…"), and use the `questionLabel: { en: "Nechama asks" }`
   feature already in the title block. If you want bilingual Nechama, we add `he` to the
   question cards selectively — but it bloats the cards.
5. **What to do with the existing `nasso-skeleton.json`?** Recommended: keep as
   `nasso-skeleton.json` for posterity (the prototype that shipped the engine feature),
   and ship the new content as `nasso.json` (no -skeleton suffix). Index lists both;
   skeleton becomes a "see how the format works" example.

## 6. Next steps once you sign off

- **Stage 3 — Source harvest** (~2 hr): fetch each chosen sheet's full content via
  `/api/sheets/<id>`, extract her cruxes, fetch every cited commentary text via Sefaria
  MCP `get_text`, stash everything in `research/parshiyot/nasso-harvest/<theme>/<year>.md`
  (one file per leaf). The harvest files become the direct input to the JSON draft.
- **Stage 4 — Draft `data/nasso.json`** (~3 hr): 13 leaves, ~150 step cards total
  (versus the skeleton's 175). Add `nasso` to `data/index.json`. Add a CSS token for
  `data-source="Nechama Leibowitz"`.
- **Stage 5 — Validate & verify** (~30 min): run the validation script, browser-verify,
  commit.

Total estimate: ~6 hours of focused work for a parsha at this depth. Repeatable weekly
once the pattern is set.
